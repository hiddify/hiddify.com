    Bug fixes in Hiddify sing-box v5

    Bugs fixed in the Hiddify fork on top of sing-box v1.15. The newest fix is at the top. Each entry names the commit that fixes it.

    All Hiddify features

    A proxy was pinged several times at once, and lowest-delay moved to a slower one

    Commit: 635d533ac

    What happened: a tap on a proxy, or a group moving its selection to it, started a test while the cycle's test of it was running, and a staged test started a second and a third attempt beside a slow first one: the proxy was pinged two or three times, and whichever test ended last wrote the result, so a late failure replaced a fresh answer and the groups left the member. After a start or a hot reload, when every result is cached, the first member to answer took the lowest-delay selection at 91 ms over a 17 ms one still being tested, and lost it a moment later; each move tested the new pick again, seconds after it was measured, and under load that second ping read 2549 ms for a 59 ms member. A member chosen while the fast one did not answer was held for the whole cycle, long after the fast one answered again. hiddify-core sent an absolute tolerance of 100 ms, which kept a 59 ms member over a 25 ms one.

    Fix: one URL test per outbound at a time: a test asked for while one runs joins it and takes its result. A staged test retries only a failed attempt, at the next mark, and skips the first mark for an outbound that worked in this run. Lowest-delay holds during a cycle only until the cycle has tested the member in use. A selection measured in the last 15 s is not tested again. The MONITORING and LOWEST log lines say why every test runs and what the group decides. hiddify-core sends a tolerance of 20 ms with a ratio of 0.3, and keeps a profile's proxy tags stable across its updates, so a hot reload keeps the unchanged proxies and their pings. Then (a85a71b1a): the first cycle's results arrive one by one and the selection still hopped at each faster one, 439 ms to 170 to 112 to 41 to 18 in forty seconds. A member that took the selection with a real ping stays for 30 s while it answers, then the best known takes it. A ping cached from the previous run decides nothing and is not shown: it only orders the members until the real pings arrive, and the app's list sorts by it (sort_delay) without showing it.

    With a large subscription, the pings changed every few seconds

    Commit: 635d533ac

    What happened: with a free subscription of a hundred or more configs, the ping the app showed changed every few seconds, up and down (29, 44, 33 ms). Three things tested the same proxies again and again. A round robin balancer told monitoring of a new selection whenever the members it rotates through changed, which is almost every result of a cycle, and each telling tested its parents (the Extra Security hop) and its next pick. A member that started working retested everything measured through each group it is in, the hop behind the selector too, whether the group used it or not. And each regular cycle moved the selection result by result. In the first minutes on an unstable network, the home screen said the selected proxy was not responding while others worked: lowest-delay stayed on the member it had cached from the last run, since a staged test's miss was hidden from the groups behind the last confirmed result, which for that member was the cached one, and was even marked as fresh.

    Fix: only a balancer that sends every connection through one member (lowest-delay) tells monitoring of a new selection. A member that starts working retests what goes through a group only when the group uses it. During a regular cycle of the schedule, lowest-delay and round robin keep the members they use while these answer, and choose with all the results when it ends (monitoring tells every group); the first cycle and one after a network change take results as they come. A miss of a member with no result of this run but the cached one is seen by the groups, so lowest-delay leaves it for a member that answers. The app shows a round robin group by a member that another replaces only when faster by more than the lowest-delay tolerance. On the Android emulator with a 160-config subscription and Extra Security, the hop's priority tests in three minutes went from 65 to 0, and the ping shown stayed the same after the first seconds.

    WARP crashed the packet tunnel when it restarted during a network change

    Commit: 00f7b6e63

    What happened: after a network change, a WARP endpoint with the scanner checks its endpoint in the background, and scans for another when it does not answer. When WARP restarted meanwhile (a refused device, a change of tunnel), the restart cleared the endpoint's scanner state while the check of the start that ended still ran. The scan read that state again to save its scores and hit a nil pointer, which ended the whole process: on iOS, the packet tunnel. The first scan at connect did the same, its probe reading the state again (h_failover.go:258 in 5.5.1): on Windows, right after connecting on a filtered network, when a refused registration restarted WARP ("register WARP masque device: context canceled").

    Fix: a check reads the scanner state of its start once and works on it to the end; the scan, the probes, the noise candidates and the scores take it from the check instead of reading the endpoint again, and the state is read and written under the endpoint's lock. A test clears the state in the middle of a scan, as a restart does; it crashed before the fix.

    A DNS rule with bypass_if_failed logged an error for every query it passed on

    Commit: b1ec924c5

    What happened: when the server of a rule with bypass_if_failed failed, the next rule answered the query, but the failure was still logged at error level. hiddify-core's Fake DNS rule uses bypass_if_failed to pass every query other than A and AAAA (HTTPS, TXT, ...) from the fake IP server to the remote rule, so a browser filled the log with "only IP queries are supported by fakeip" errors.

    Fix: a failure that the next rule takes over is logged at debug level. A failure with no bypass is still an error. Two tests check both cases.

    A masterdns outbound used one of several resolvers on the same IP

    Commit: 0e8ed7f69

    What happened: with smart_pool off, the outbound gives its resolvers to the MasterDnsVPN client, whose library entry point kept one resolver per IP. Resolvers on one IP with different ports (127.0.0.1:5301, 127.0.0.1:5302, or a server answering on more than one port) became a single resolver, so the client's balancer and packet duplication had nothing to spread over.

    Fix: MasterDnsVPN (the Hiddify fork, 62e94f1) keeps each IP and port as a resolver of its own; the resolver file loader keeps its old behaviour. A test checks one IP with three ports.

    TUN: DNS stalled the whole tunnel

    Commit: d098fded1

    What happened: DNS queries caught by the TUN were handled on the tunnel's packet loop. With a DNS server behind a dead proxy, each query held every packet of the tunnel for 10 seconds.

    Fix: Each query is handled separately, at most 256 at once.

    libbox: Connections was not safe for concurrent use

    Commit: 767a337ef

    What happened: A platform app applies connection events on its command client's thread, and its UI filters, sorts and lists the same Connections object on the main thread. Nothing guarded the map and slices they share, so the connection list could crash the app or show broken entries.

    Fix: Every exported method of Connections now takes a lock.

    Balancer: sticky sessions landed on dead members

    Commit: 299f7c824

    What happened: When the member remembered for a session was dead, sticky-sessions picked again with a seed that was often identical on every retry. After the last retry it used the pick without checking it, so about half of the new sessions went to a dead member.

    Fix: Each retry draws with a new seed. If no retry finds a live member, the first live member is used. A dead member is used only when none is alive.

    Balancer: untested members counted as the fastest ones

    Commit: 26038512b

    What happened: consistent-hashing and sticky-sessions read the raw test delay. A member that was never tested has a delay of 0, so it looked like the fastest member and got traffic. A delay restored from the cache was trusted as if it were fresh.

    Fix: These strategies now use the same adjusted delay as lowest-delay and round-robin. An untested member counts as timed out, and a cached delay gets a penalty.

    Balancer: a large delay ratio overflowed

    Commit: 9352492aa

    What happened: The largest acceptable delay is the smallest delay × delay_acceptable_ratio. It was stored in a 16-bit number, which wraps past 65,535 ms. A cached delay gets a 20,000 ms penalty, so with a ratio of 3 two cached members gave a limit of about 9,000 ms, and round-robin then used only one of them.

    Fix: The limit is computed in one place and capped just below the timeout delay. A timed-out member is still never accepted.

    Balancer: round-robin reported no selected member

    Commit: c4c581f38

    What happened: A round-robin balancer always reported an empty current member, so the app and the Clash API showed nothing selected.

    Fix: It reports the member the next TCP connection goes to.

    Balancer: closing did not stop the worker

    Commit: e3a6b22fa

    What happened: Close did nothing, so the worker that follows URL-test results kept running until the whole service stopped. After a configuration reload, the old balancer kept updating itself.

    Fix: Close stops the worker.

    SSH: no background connection after start

    Commit: 69fa3a66e

    What happened: The SSH outbound connects in the background after start and reconnects when the connection drops, so it is ready and tested before any traffic. After the move to sing-box v1.15's startup stages, this step was never run. The outbound stayed not ready, and the URL test skipped it, until traffic arrived.

    Fix: The background connection starts again once the service has started.

    Startup: some outbounds were never started, and the balancer crashed

    Commit: ac35f63e4

    What happened: sing-box v1.15 starts components in stages. Outbounds written for the older startup (balancer, psiphon, dnstt and gooserelay) were only closed at shutdown and never started. A balancer that was never started has no strategy, and its first connection crashed the whole process.

    Fix: These outbounds go through the startup stages again: preparation, start, and the step after the service has started.

    WARP: an incomplete profile crashed the process

    Commit: 35779caa3

    What happened: The WARP endpoint starts in the background, so any crash there takes the whole process down. Three cases crashed it:

    • the profile request failed and there was no outbound to retry through;
    • the profile (cached, written in the config, or from the API) had no peers or no ports;
    • the profile had no interface addresses.

    Fix: Each case now reports an error. The peer address and port are picked with checks, in this order: the configured server and server_port, a port listed in the profile, or the port in the host name. A missing address family is skipped.

    TUIC: a misspelled UDP relay mode was accepted

    Commit: 36861907a

    What happened: Any udp_relay_mode other than quic, such as quick or Native, silently meant native.

    Fix: An empty value or native means native, and quic means QUIC. Any other value fails when the configuration loads.

    XHTTP: lost dial errors and accepted rejected uploads

    Commit: fee26e298

    What happened:

    • When the download stream could not connect, the error was lost. The caller got a reader that never returned data, so the connection hung until it timed out.
    • On HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, an upload the server rejected (any status other than 200) counted as sent.
    • The closed flag was read and written from different goroutines without synchronization.

    Fix: The dial error reaches the caller. On HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, a status other than 200 is an error, as in Xray. The closed flag is atomic.

    REALITY: a long short_id crashed the process

    Commit: f6f378e81

    What happened: A REALITY short_id is at most 8 bytes, which is 16 hex digits. A longer value overflowed the buffer it was decoded into and crashed the process, on both the client and the server.

    Fix: A short_id longer than 16 hex digits is a configuration error.

    VLESS: a failed handshake leaked the connection

    Commit: ea3c70fa5

    What happened: When the TLS handshake or the VLESS encryption handshake failed, the underlying TCP connection was never closed. A failed encryption handshake also replaced the connection with an empty value, which could crash later code. This applied to both TCP and UDP.

    Fix: The original connection is closed on any handshake failure, and it is replaced only after a successful handshake.

    Trojan: a disabled TLS block crashed the process

    Commit: 27c97f0d8

    What happened: A Trojan outbound with a tls block where enabled is false built a TLS dialer with no TLS settings, and the first connection crashed the process.

    Fix: The TLS dialer is built only when TLS is enabled.
